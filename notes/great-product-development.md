# Great Product Development

**Discover** (divergent and empirical)— get close to a real problem before committing to a solution
* Talk to users — but interrogate behavior, not opinions — backward-looking and specific, You're mining for stories, not predictions
* Watch people do the thing
* Look at the data you already have
* Run small, fast probes

**Frame** (convergent and a decision) — define the problem sharply, key assumptions to test, and decide what success looks like
**Build** — make the smallest thing that tests your riskiest assumption, not the most complete thing
**Learn** — ship it, measure against your success criteria, and let the result update your beliefs, including killing things that don't work.

judgment about which uncertainty to attack first

Marty Cagan calls these the four risks worth de-risking up front: value (will they want it?), usability (can they use it?), feasibility (can we build it?), and viability (does it work for the business?)
