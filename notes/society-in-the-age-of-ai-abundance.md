# Society in the Age of AI Abundance

From a late-night voice memo. The fullest statement yet of the recurring "what should society look like" question — the form-giver faculty aimed at economics.

## The core vision

**AI displacing jobs is a net positive — if the freed time is designed well.** If AI absorbs the menial work, humans get their time back for genuinely human needs. But the design matters: not a free-fall into hobbies.

- **Everyone works ~20 hours/week contributing to their community.** People still hold *vocations* and take their role in the community seriously — e.g. signing up to volunteer at a school — rather than playing golf 20 hours a week.
- **Heavy insistence on fostering individual growth *through* contribution.** Contribution is the growth mechanism, not just the output.
- **Some roles shouldn't be replaced at all:** therapists, teachers — the relational core stays human.

## The supporting critiques

- **Wages.** Hourly wages are the wrong unit — pay should be commensurate with contribution (riffed on "commensurate with age" as a playing-field leveler). The core thing people actually need from a job: knowing they'll **be taken care of**.
- **Wealth distribution needs a rethink.** "Capitalism was invented ~250–300 years ago; do people really think we can't figure out a better system?"
- **An AI pause.** A "peace treaty" pausing AI development ~1 year so the world can get its head around the transition — not anti-building, just sequencing.
