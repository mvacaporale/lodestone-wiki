# Tim Ferriss — Daily Prioritization Process

A method for deciding what to work on so the day has real leverage, not just volume. The core bet: **one critical task done beats ten important tasks half-attempted.** Ten "important" items on a list guarantees nothing meaningful gets finished.

Source: [tim.blog/2013/11/03/productivity-hacks](https://tim.blog/2013/11/03/productivity-hacks/)

## The exercise (run it in the morning)

1. **Wake at least an hour before you need to be at a computer.** No email at this stage — it hijacks the frame before you've set it.
2. **Sit with pen and paper** (tea optional). Analog on purpose.
3. **Write down the 3–5 things causing you the most anxiety or discomfort.** These are usually the things you've been postponing — and usually the most important. Discomfort is the signal, not noise.
4. **Filter each one through the two questions** (below). Keep only items that earn at least one "yes."
5. **Pick ONE** and block **2–3 consecutive hours** for it — and only it.

## The two questions

For each candidate task, ask:

1. **"If this were the only thing I accomplished today, would I be satisfied with my day?"**
2. **"Will moving this forward make all the other to-do's unimportant or easier to knock off later?"**

Question 1 tests whether it's *worth* it. Question 2 tests whether it's the *domino* — the thing that collapses or shrinks the rest of the list. The best task answers yes to both.

## The rule that makes it work

> **"This is ONE BLOCK OF TIME. Cobbling together 10 minutes here and there to add up to 120 minutes does not work."**

One protected 2–3 hour block on the single task. The uninterrupted stretch is the whole point — fragmented time doesn't produce the same outcome, even if the minutes add up.

## Why it works

It rejects volume-based productivity. The failure mode isn't laziness; it's a long list of legitimately important tasks that diffuses focus so nothing lands. This process forces a single point of concentration on the item with the most leverage, and protects the conditions for actually doing it.

Pairs with the Compass formula: *Results = clarity × concentration + uninterrupted time.*
