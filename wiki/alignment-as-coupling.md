# The Alignment Arc

This cluster is the spine of the collection: a sustained argument that alignment — of cells into bodies, of people into societies, of AIs with humans — is one phenomenon, and that its mechanism is **coupling**. Michaelangelo starts from the "organic alignment" thesis of Emmett Shear's Softmax lab (alignment should be grown, not imposed; when parts cooperate tightly enough, "the 'we' becomes an 'I'"), then rebuilds it from the ground up through evolutionary biology, cybernetics, and complexity science. The through-line: a part is aligned with a whole when its fate is genuinely bound to the whole's — through a shared, consequence-carrying signal that stays honest — not when it is commanded from above.

Along the way the argument accumulates precise machinery. From Maynard Smith and Szathmáry's major evolutionary transitions it takes the one hard problem every transition solves: suppressing competition among the parts. From Michael Levin it takes the cognitive light cone and the idea that goals live in the coupling between units, not inside any one of them. From Ashby's requisite variety it derives why centralized control cannot work and what the center's one irreducible job actually is — arbitration, collapsing conflicting local votes into a single committed course. And it ends with instruments: causal emergence to detect *when* a higher-level self has formed, and the Free Energy Principle for *what that self does and why it persists*.

**A note on the numbering.** The numbered notes are chapters 1, 2, 3, 6, and 7 of a longer private sequence. Chapters 4 ("Society — Goals & the Economy") and 5 ("Criticality & the Coordination Spectrum") are unpublished, and the published notes refer to them. Notes 6 and 7 summarize what they lean on, so the arc remains readable — just know there's a gap where the argument turns from biology to society.
