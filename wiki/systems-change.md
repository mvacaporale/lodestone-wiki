# How Systems Get Stuck — and Change

If [Society and Systems](society-and-systems.md) says our systems optimize the wrong things, this cluster asks the harder follow-up: *why do they keep doing it even when everyone can see the problem?* The shared answer across these notes is dynamical: rigid systems sit in deep **attractor basins** held in place by self-reinforcing feedbacks, and reforms that push on a variable the feedbacks don't govern simply roll back downhill. Michaelangelo works this out most concretely in education — why 13,000 nominally independent school districts all look the same, why "Portrait of a Graduate" reforms stall, and why the admissions metric functions as the master energy sink the whole K-12 system drains into.

The flip side is a theory of how stuck systems *do* change. The longest note in the collection argues that one computational signature — precision-weighted prediction error, in Free Energy Principle terms — governs model updating from traumatized brains to stagnant organizations to industrial civilization, and that updating everywhere requires the same five conditions: optimal disequilibrium, a holding environment, energy surplus, an available alternative model, and mechanisms that register errors instead of suppressing them. The energy-sinks framework turns this into strategy: build *illegible* energy sources the incumbent system can't tax or co-opt, and stay illegible until you're a strong enough attractor to survive contact.

A short foundational note on the definition of information rounds out the toolkit — information as the structure that constrains a system's possible states, the substrate all this feedback and signaling runs on.
